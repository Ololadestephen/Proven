THE PROVEN GUIDE

Post once.
Inspect the result.

Proven handles work with executable acceptance conditions. This guide explains the path from those conditions to a recorded delivery result, and what remains trusted along the way.

How a job moves

  1. Post and fund once. The buyer commits the public format, check and reference hashes, payment, checking budget and attempt fees in one transaction. The board creates the escrow job.
  2. Review and challenge. A bid review can block entry but cannot pay anyone. The relay sends the committed check and reference for its planted-fake challenge. A rejected check leaves no worker authorised to claim the job.
  3. Claim. A worker claims an allowed, checked job. The board fixes that worker in the escrow; the buyer does not sign another funding or linking transaction.
  4. Deliver. The assigned worker records the delivery hash and hands over exactly those bytes privately. Changed bytes do not match.
  5. Verify. The worker or relay can request execution using the prepaid checking budget. Only escrow’s private promise callback records a verdict; model output never decides payment.
  6. Settle and return storage. A pass permits payment to the worker. A failed or expired attempt can reopen the job within its limits; closing returns the buyer’s remaining funds. The relay syncs ended bids and asks both contracts to return unused storage. The measured remainder stays as evidence. Inspect confirmed transfer state rather than inferring a payout from a pending state.

A failed run is not a failed delivery.

A promise failure, timeout, malformed result, oversized output or mismatched binding produces no verdict. It must not become a pass or fail. The job can be retried within its window and then reach its expiry/refund path.

A pending payout is also different from a confirmed transfer. The app labels the recorded contract state and shows what is still pending.

What remains trusted

  • OutLayer’s contract and operator. Registered worker keys and approved measurements are part of the verdict’s trust path. An individual run’s quote is not verified on chain.
  • The relay’s handling of private inputs. The relay can read or withhold private plaintext. It cannot post a verdict. Changed texts are refused when they do not match the job’s hashes.
  • Custody and keystore services. A buyer or worker may use a restricted custody wallet or scoped key. Its policy must permit the actual one-signature board call. These restrictions do not remove trust in the service or host.
  • Contract upgrade keys. Whoever controls the escrow account’s full-access key can change its code.
  • Public data providers. The viewer relies on the configured network’s NEAR RPC and OutLayer API. The separate historical evidence reader reads recorded testnet jobs. Neither verifies a NEAR light-client proof.

What an attestation panel means

“Bindings matched” means the fetched fields match the stored job, verifier, input, output and secret-reference commitments. The page does not independently check the Intel signature chain or worker-measurement approval.

Selected recorded runs have passed independent command-line signature checks, documented in the project README. That does not independently establish the private plaintext or reproducible builds.

Where Proven stops

Subjective work is outside this flow. Proven does not decide whether an essay is good, settle a dispute by opinion, or establish correctness beyond the committed check.

useproven.xyz is an unaudited, owner-funded mainnet deployment. The owner holds a single key that can upgrade the contracts. The board enforces its buyer list on posting. The app reads access from that board and limits from the escrow instead of assuming a fixed cap. The recorded examples on this page are historical scripted testnet jobs. Cross-chain payouts are not implemented. The relay operator can read private inputs; this page does not independently verify their handling.
